01

Choosing k

k is not assumed — it's selected by scanning k=2–8 and maximizing silhouette score. It peaked at k=2, not the k=3 the synthetic archetypes were built from: the algorithm merged "moderate" and "severe" into one broad elevated-risk group.

02

The two groups

Per-cluster means for the features that separate them most, plus the modal social-determinant profile for each group.

03

What actually separates them

A Random Forest trained to predict cluster label from the original features reaches 98.0% cross-validated accuracy — the split is a real, learnable pattern, driven by a mix of clinical severity and access-to-care, not one or the other.

04

Equity audit

How strongly does cluster membership associate with socioeconomic variables that weren't meant to be the whole story?

Finding: cluster membership is strongly associated with income bracket, employment status, and insurance status (Cramér's V ≈ 0.5–0.6). This "elevated risk" group is arguably as much a resource-access group as a clinical-severity group — a real consideration for how any grouping like this should (and shouldn't) inform care decisions.
